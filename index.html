About

Who I am

I'm Aswin D Menon, a graduate student at the University of Michigan, Ann Arbor, pursuing an MS in Aerospace Engineering. My research, conducted at the Distributed Autonomous Systems and Control (DASC) Lab under Prof. Dimitra Panagou, focuses on safety-critical control for autonomous multi-robot systems — spanning backup safety filters (Backup CBF, MPS, gatekeeper), perception-limited exploration (SEAMLiS), and event-triggered algorithms that reduce computational cost while preserving safety guarantees. I am also interested in motion planning, autonomous navigation, and applying advanced control theory to real robotic platforms. I am committed to pushing the frontiers of safe, intelligent autonomy through rigorous research and innovative design.

Graduate Research | Distributed Autonomous Systems and Control Lab

Prof. Dimitra Panagou | September 2025 – Present

1. Comparative Review of Backup Safety Filters
  • Conducted a unified comparative analysis of Backup Control Barrier Functions (CBFs), Model Predictive Shielding (MPS), and gatekeeper safety filters, formulating theoretical proofs that explicitly connect their filter-inactive sets.
  • Architected reach-avoid and dynamic highway overtake simulation scenarios to benchmark failure modes and evaluate the conservatism of each backup policy.
2. SEAMLiS — Perception-Limited Multi-Robot Exploration
  • Helped in development of the SEAMLiS modular safety framework to guarantee collision-free decentralized multi-robot exploration under finite sensing ranges and limited fields of view.
  • Implemented the algorithmic architecture and designed randomized multi-robot simulation scenarios to rigorously benchmark collision avoidance and coverage efficiency against standard visibility-promoting baselines.
3. Event-Triggered Gatekeeper (ET-GK)
  • Engineered the Event-Triggered Gatekeeper (ET-GK) algorithm, introducing a computationally lightweight trigger metric to bypass expensive online switching-time validations while strictly preserving recursive feasibility.
  • Designed unicycle robot navigation simulations in cluttered environments to validate the algorithm, successfully proving a 64% reduction in mean computational time compared to standard time-triggered approaches.
